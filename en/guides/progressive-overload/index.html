Tracking progressive overload starts with comparable logs.

Comparing workouts means more than finding the largest number. A workout log helps distinguish weights, repetitions, variations and conditions. This guide explains logging and the rules in the current VALKR app.

What to record for a useful comparison

Record the exact exercise, working load, repetitions per set and date. For holds, time is the relevant value. Make equipment or variation changes identifiable. A machine reading is not automatically comparable with a free weight.

A logging example, not a training prescription: one session records 3 × 8 at 40 kg, the next 3 × 9 at the same load. Under comparable conditions, more repetitions have been logged. At 42.5 kg with fewer repetitions, load increased; a single number does not describe the whole change.

How VALKR uses previous performance

The workout interface places previous performance alongside current targets. Load progression in guided plans can increase, maintain or reduce the working load you actually used. The decision depends on whether set targets were met or missed.

This logic is rule-based. It is not evidence of automatic AI programming for every workout. Custom sessions, bodyweight progression and guided plans have different targets. Consider the specific exercise and the training path you are using.

Read records in context

A personal record marks a best performance in your stored history. It does not independently explain your readiness today or validate your technique. A calculated one-rep max remains an estimate. Different exercises and equipment need separate comparisons.

For a practical system, connect your plan with accurate set logging and review exercise history later. VALKR provides these paths in the current product. The app is preparing for its public Android launch; the waitlist provides launch-access information.
