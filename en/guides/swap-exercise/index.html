VALKR GUIDE

Equipment busy? Adjust your session deliberately.

You arrive at the gym and the planned equipment is occupied. VALKR supports exercise swaps and editable sessions. This guide explains the workflow and why a replacement movement needs its own log and an appropriate load.

1. Find the affected exercise slot

Open the session and identify the affected exercise. Swapping changes the movement; reordering initially moves the same entry. If the equipment will become available soon, reordering may be more appropriate than choosing a different movement. Decide which approach fits your session.

VALKR lets you reorder, swap and add exercises within a session. Check the training path shown: a guided plan and a custom workout have different targets. Adding another entry does not automatically replace the original exercise.

2. Use “Swap exercise”

Choose “Swap exercise” for the affected slot. The picker offers exercise search and filters; the catalogue covers equipment and muscle areas. Look for a movement whose equipment you can actually use. A name alone is not enough to establish that two exercises are equivalent.

Equipment profiles support gym, home and travel training. Match a profile to your actual training location. Hiding an equipment type does not mean the app checks whether someone is currently using a machine: equipment selection and real-time availability are different information.

3. Review the replacement and target

After the swap, check the new name, set count and displayed target. Also consider whether you know the movement and whether its demonstration matches the equipment. An automatically displayed value does not confirm that you should use the same load as for another movement.

For example, a machine pulling exercise and a cable variation may have different leverage, movement paths and load displays. Forty kilograms on machine A cannot automatically be treated as forty kilograms on machine B. This example explains a comparison limit; it does not prescribe a replacement exercise or load.

4. Log the movement you actually performed

Record your actual sets under the selected replacement movement. Do not retain the original name after performing a different exercise. Otherwise a later comparison could show an increase or decrease when the first thing that changed was the movement itself.

Use notes when needed to record equipment or execution details. The replacement exercise’s own history is more useful for choosing a load than a record from another variation. Correct a mistaken set within its existing entry instead of logging the same effort twice.

5. Check the next session

Check which movement appears in the next session. VALKR supports dated adjustments and different training paths; do not assume that a change for today is always a permanent change. Your saved history should still identify the movement you performed on each date.

Pain is a different situation from occupied equipment. The swap function does not assess an injury or certify that a replacement movement is pain-free. This guide covers session organisation and logging, not individual medical or therapeutic advice.
